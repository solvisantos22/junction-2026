# Interactive agent benchmark landscape

Research snapshot: **13 November 2026**. Scope: prior environments and what they imply for an AfterQuery “Break the Agent” submission. This is research and provisional design advice, not evidence that any of our three ideas already defeats a current model. Live-page access dates are distinguished from publication and run dates below. Only primary research, author pages, and official repositories support the findings.

**The strongest opportunity is a compact environment with an independently credible reference and a diagnosed failure mechanism. “Long horizon,” “resource management,” and “agents forget things” are insufficient differentiators.** Contemporary counterevidence matters: Vending-Bench 2 reports substantial positive balances, and AutoVend reports a current model outperforming its inventory-control reference. Conversely, a high aggregate reward can coexist with coordination errors. We should measure our proposed problem directly before choosing it on the strength of earlier failures. [Vending-Bench 2](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2), [AutoVend, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36980v2)

## What changed the interpretation

**The business results are a warning against stale narratives.** Vending-Bench 2 adds adversarial suppliers, delivery disruption, refunds, notes and reminders. Its prompt declares context trimming around 69,000 tokens. The displayed rows do not expose exact inference settings, run timestamps, or the definition of their uncertainty notation; preserve that limitation. Its roughly $63,000 “good strategy” figure is an estimate, not a demonstrated human/reference rollout. [Methods and ceiling discussion](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2)

AutoVend's reference combines demand-based inventory targets, periodic replenishment and lead-time accounting using observable information. The agent gets SQL and persistent notes. [§§3.4–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36980v2) **Interpretation:** repeated trials in one world do not establish generalization. Our routing constraint and mid-route disruption could still distinguish ColdChain, but “perishable stock plus tools” does not.

**Scaffolds can remove the apparent problem.** Project Vend's authors identify enforced product-research procedures, inventory purchase-price visibility and better research tools as useful changes. Adding a CEO agent was not clearly useful and might have harmed performance. Since the model and scaffold changed together, phase two is not a controlled attribution of improvement to either component. [“Tools” and “What actually worked?”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2) **Interpretation:** supply a competent agent with readable state, arithmetic/code access where permitted, and memory before celebrating its failure. A comparison against an intentionally awkward interface would be fragile.

BALROG's original study used direct zero-shot action prediction conditioned on history and explicitly separated model backbones from inference strategies. The April 2025 paper reports **32.64%** average language-only progress for Claude 3.5 Sonnet; the current table contains much stronger submissions. [Original protocol and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13543v2), [current table](https://balrogai.com/) The September Astra row lacks the checkmark displayed beside many older rows; its exact scaffold and verification status were not established here. **Interpretation:** use that row as evidence to investigate, not as a fully controlled model-only improvement measurement.

**Failure attribution deserves its own experiment.** τ²-bench compares normal dual-control interaction with a no-user variant and an oracle-plan variant. These isolate communication from planning better than one headline score. Its workflow-document ablation can help one setting while hurting another. [§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07982v1) Subsequent Sierra releases change evaluators and simulator defaults, so the original paper's numbers cannot simply be relabeled as current. [Official releases](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench/releases) The same principle applies to our reference: a reference given future disturbances is an oracle upper bound, not the fair online competitor.

## Ranked lessons for the hackathon

These are proposed decisions derived from the evidence, not measured outcomes for our environments.

1. **Pilot the strongest permitted agent before committing to the story.** Use the actual evaluation harness, model identifier, reasoning setting and tool budget. Include a competent scaffold. A failure observed only with an older model, missing memory, or disabled arithmetic is unlikely to survive judging. Vending-Bench 2 and AutoVend make this the first experiment, not a finishing check.

2. **Build one controlled source of difficulty.** For ColdChain, compare identical deliveries with and without a disclosed mid-episode disruption; measure quality after replanning. For BatchPilot, compare instantaneous and delayed response while keeping observations and targets fixed. For RecoveryRoom, compare ordinary restart with interrupted work that must be reconciled. The goal is to explain why a gap occurs rather than merely produce one.

3. **Make the reference fair, executable and inspectable.** Give it the same observable state and action interface. Record every event. Label any full-future-information solver separately. A reference can use a purpose-built algorithm; it must not secretly obtain unrevealed events or state. A short rollout with an understandable optimal or near-optimal policy is more persuasive than a speculative human ceiling.

4. **Score outcomes and continuing obligations.** Count useful delivered goods, acceptable finished batches, or uniquely completed jobs, together with relevant costs and irreversible violations. Continue to a common evaluation horizon after stop/no-op where obligations remain. Log partial progress for diagnosis, but do not let inventory, warm-up activity, or superficially healthy services substitute for completed useful work. See the companion [reward and evaluation review](reward-and-evaluation.md) for verifier details.

5. **Make independence and randomness explicit.** Repeating an agent three times on one seed measures something different from three newly generated scenarios. Report both the prescribed repeated runs and, if affordable, a few held-out scenario seeds. Freeze the generator, simulator, prompts and reference before final evaluation. Save infrastructure errors separately; do not silently count them as cognitive failures or omit them from the denominator.

6. **Treat novelty as a mechanism, not a theme.** Retail, games, customer support, desktop workflows and company simulations already have extensive prior art in the sources above. Our defensible contribution could be a fast, deterministic, replayable instance where delayed consequences or coupled commitments expose a clear reference gap. None of these papers tells us how many Junction teams will choose a theme; benchmark popularity and local competition are separate unknowns.

## Implications for the shortlist

**ColdChain:** strongest narrative fit for an understandable constrained-planning comparison, but the landscape supplies substantial counterevidence to an easy win. Retain it only if a pilot shows a robust gap with replanning and fair tools. Use explicit capacity, time, spoilage and disruption semantics; avoid relying on a generic “resource management is hard” claim.

**BatchPilot:** offers a more concentrated mechanism—actions with delayed consequences—but this survey does not establish a current frontier weakness in control. Its value depends on the control review and a pilot in which the agent may use the same disclosed dynamics and permitted computation as the reference.

**RecoveryRoom:** can expose an appealing distinction between restoring availability and preserving work. The central challenge is verifier design: a shortcut that deletes pending jobs must not look successful. Keep the service model small enough that every lost or duplicated job is explainable from the trace. The evidence above supports this evaluation style, not a guaranteed performance gap.
